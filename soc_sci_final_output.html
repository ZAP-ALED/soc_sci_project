Think Before You Prompt

A Call to Action for Ethical, Responsible, and Critical Use of Artificial Intelligence

Creative Output: Call to Action

Artificial Intelligence is no longer a distant future, it is embedded in how we study, work, communicate, and make decisions. From generative AI tools to automated decision systems, AI increasingly shapes what we know, what we trust, and how power circulates in society.

Think before you prompt. Question before you trust. Use AI responsibly.

Call to Action

Four Pillars of Responsible AI Use

🧠
Critical Thinking

Use AI to support learning, not replace thinking and authorship.

Critical thinking in AI use means evaluating outputs rather than accepting them at face value. This includes questioning sources, checking assumptions embedded in models, and recognizing the limits of automation in tasks that require judgment, creativity, or ethical reasoning.

⚖️
Ethics & Bias

Interrogate AI outputs for bias, errors, and unequal impacts.

AI systems inherit biases from training data and design choices. Ethical engagement requires awareness of whose values are embedded in algorithms and who may be marginalized by automated decision-making.

📚
Integrity

Respect academic honesty, attribution, and human labor.

Integrity involves transparency in authorship and responsible use of AI in academic and professional contexts. AI should assist learning—not replace accountability or original work.

🔍
Transparency

Advocate for explainable and accountable AI systems.

Transparency demands that AI systems be explainable and auditable. Users should understand how outputs are generated and who is responsible when systems fail or cause harm.

Background and Rationale

Why this topic and this creative output?

I chose artificial intelligence as an advocacy topic because AI technologies increasingly influence education, employment, governance, and public discourse. As a Computer Science student at the University of the Philippines Diliman, I encounter AI not only as a user-facing tool but as a system designed, trained, and deployed through technical decisions that carry social and ethical consequences. While AI promises efficiency and innovation, it also raises concerns about misinformation, academic dishonesty, algorithmic bias, data exploitation, and the erosion of critical thinking. These issues affect students and citizens directly, yet public understanding of AI often lags behind its rapid adoption.

I chose a website as the creative output because it reflects the very environment where AI tools are most commonly encountered—online and within digital platforms. From a computing perspective, a website allows the integration of structure, interactivity, and design to communicate ideas responsibly and effectively. It enables the advocacy to combine explanation, reflection, and action in a format that is accessible, shareable, and scalable, while also modeling ethical digital communication through clarity, transparency, and intentional user experience design.

Thought process and construction

The central idea of this project is captured in the phrase “Think Before You Prompt.” This slogan reframes AI use as a moral and intellectual decision rather than a purely technical one. I structured the website to first contextualize the issue, then present a clear call to action, and finally explain the reasoning behind the advocacy.

The content avoids alarmist language and instead emphasizes agency and responsibility. The design is intentionally minimal to reduce distraction and reflect the value of thoughtful engagement. The bullet-pointed call to action translates abstract ethical concerns into concrete practices that students and everyday users can adopt immediately.

Course concepts applied

This work applies course concepts on public intellectualism, ethics of communication, and critical technological literacy. It positions the creator as a mediator between expert knowledge and public understanding, translating complex debates about AI into accessible language. The advocacy also draws from framing theory by presenting AI use as a shared social responsibility rather than an individual moral failing.

Additionally, the project reflects lessons on audience-centered advocacy and responsible knowledge production, emphasizing reflection, accountability, and civic engagement in technology-driven societies.
